🔴 Broker Controls All Cash
The entire 82L revenue goes to the broker first. They decide how much you get. If they face financial trouble, delay payments, or underreport profits — you have no independent access to revenue. Your 2 machines are also now in their hands. This is the #1 risk of this deal.
🔴 Workers' Salary: Who Pays?
"Thread, electricity, installments, and other expenses" — does "other" include your workers' salaries (3.22L/month)? If not, you're left paying salary from your profit share. Biggest ambiguity in the deal. Must clarify before anything else.
🟡 60L Billing Claim — Verify It
Their 4 machines billing 60L means 15L/machine vs your 11L/machine. That's 36% more output per machine. Could be real (higher-head machines, denser designs) but could also be projected/aspirational. Ask to see actual client orders or production data before committing.
🟡 Your Machines in New Space
Moving your machines to their rental space means you lose physical access control. If the partnership breaks down, you'll need to negotiate machine removal. Ensure the written agreement includes your right to remove machines on 30-days notice without penalty.
🟡 No Agreed Fixed Amount Yet
They mentioned a "fixed amount" but haven't said what it is. You also don't know if the 25.9% is of GROSS revenue or NET profit. The difference is enormous — 25.9% of 82L = 21.2L vs 25.9% of 32L = 8.3L. Get exact formula in writing.
🟡 Management Unpaid
You'll be running a 6-machine operation physically and mentally. Your 25.9% only reflects your machine ownership — NOT your management work. Running their 4 machines for free means you're essentially providing free skilled labor. This needs separate compensation.
🟠 Your Owned Space Left Empty
If you move your machines to the new rental space, your own purchased space sits empty. That's an opportunity cost. Consider whether you could rent that space to someone else and earn additional income (2–3L/month or more depending on size and location).
⚪ Market Dependency
You'll become fully dependent on their client base for all 6 machines. If their contracts slow down or they face payment issues from their own clients, your income drops immediately. Build a clause requiring minimum guaranteed payment regardless of market conditions.
Negotiation Checklist — Before You Agree to Anything
📋 Walk into the negotiation knowing your baseline: you currently earn 5.22L/month with full control. Any deal that gives you less than that — with MORE responsibility — is a bad deal. Use the checklist below.

🔴 Must-Have (Non-Negotiable)

  • !
    SALARY CLARITY: Confirm workers' salary (3.22L/month) is included in "other expenses" paid by them. Get this in writing. If they say no, the deal terms need to change significantly.
  • !
    PROFIT FORMULA IN WRITING: Your 25.9% must be defined as: Net Profit = Total Revenue – (All documented expenses). Not 25.9% of revenue. Get the exact formula documented with examples.
  • !
    MINIMUM GUARANTEE: Regardless of profits or market conditions, you must receive a minimum floor of 5.5L/month (your current earnings). If profit share falls below this, they top it up. This is your protection.
  • !
    MACHINE RETURN CLAUSE: Written agreement that your 2 machines can be removed within 30 days notice without any claim, penalty, or compensation from the party.
  • !
    MONTHLY ACCOUNTS: You must receive a full monthly statement showing: total billing for all 6 machines, itemized expenses, net profit calculation, and your computed share. On or before the 10th of next month.

🟡 Should Have (Important)

  • →
    MANAGEMENT FEE: For running their 4 machines, you deserve separate compensation. Propose: 2.5% of their machine billing = 1.5L/month. This is standard for a unit manager role. Start at 3% (1.8L) and settle at 2–2.5%. This is completely separate from your 25.9% ownership share.
  • →
    VERIFY THEIR 60L CLAIM: Before agreeing, ask to see 2–3 months of confirmed order books, client contracts, or previous billing records from their brokerage. If they're serious, they'll show it.
  • →
    BANK TRANSFER ONLY: All payments to you should come via bank transfer (not cash). This creates a paper trail and protects you if there's a dispute later.
  • →
    EXPENSE AUDIT RIGHT: You should be able to verify expenses. Propose that both parties agree on a list of acceptable expense categories and their documentation requirements (receipts, invoices).
  • →
    HIRED WORKERS CLARITY: Define who is responsible for hiring/firing workers, EOBI/social security, and salary disputes. If you're managing, you need authority — not just responsibility.

🟢 Nice to Have

  • +
    YOUR EMPTY SPACE: If you move machines, your own space becomes unused. Explore renting it out. Even 1.5–2L/month in additional rental income would be significant.
  • +
    ESCALATION CLAUSE: As production increases, your management fee should increase proportionally. Add a clause that management fee scales with billing growth.
  • +
    TRIAL PERIOD: Propose a 3-month trial period before the formal partnership deed is finalized. This protects both parties and lets you verify their billing claims with real numbers.

Final Analysis and Recommendation
⚖️ Overall Verdict: Conditionally Favorable
The deal is potentially excellent financially — but only if the terms are properly negotiated and documented. Right now the offer is loosely defined and has gaps that could hurt you significantly.

On paper, you go from earning 5.22L/month to potentially 9–11L/month — that's nearly double your income with zero out-of-pocket expenses. Your 5.17L installment burden disappears. You no longer worry about thread stock or market payments. That's a real and significant benefit.

But the core problem is this: you do 100% of the work, they hold 100% of the cash. That power imbalance must be corrected through written agreements, minimum guarantees, and a separate management fee. Without those, this deal could easily leave you with less income than now — and more stress.
What Makes It Worth Doing
✅ Installment Relief = Immediate Gain
5.17L/month in machine installments gone. That alone is huge. Right now installments eat 30% of your revenue. In the new deal, that burden transfers completely to the party — and your machines get fully paid through their money.
✅ Thread Stock Headache = Gone
Thread procurement (5.71L/month) requires working capital management. In the new deal, they handle all stock payments. You focus purely on running the unit — no cash flow juggling for materials.
✅ Scale = Higher Income
3x scale (2 → 6 machines) with your existing skills. Your 25.9% of a much larger operation beats 100% of a small one — IF the billing claims are real.
What Could Break It
❌ If salary NOT covered by them
Your net income could drop below current levels. Walk away or renegotiate completely. Do not proceed without salary being explicitly included in "other expenses."
❌ If nothing is in writing
Verbal agreements for a business of this size are unacceptable. At 82L/month combined billing, a dispute without documentation could cost you everything — including your machines.
❌ If their 60L is aspirational
If actual billing is 30–40L instead of 60L, your share drops to 4–5L — less than your current income, but now you have 3x the management burden. Verify before committing.
Action Plan — What To Do Next
  • 1
    Clarify Salary Question — Ask immediately: "Jab aap kehte hain expenses covered — kya isme workers ki salary bhi aati hai?" If yes, get it documented. If no, renegotiate the fixed amount upward.
  • 2
    Ask for Proof of 60L Billing — Request 2–3 months of their existing client orders or production records. Serious partners won't hesitate to show this.
  • 3
    Propose Management Fee — Tell them: "Jo 4 machines aap lagaenge, unhe run karna meri responsibility hai — uskey liye alag compensation chahiye. 2.5% of their billing per month."
  • 4
    Demand Minimum Guarantee — "I need a minimum guaranteed amount of 5.5L/month — regardless of how the market goes. This is my floor, non-negotiable."
  • 5
    Propose 3-Month Trial First — Don't move machines permanently until you see 3 months of actual billing. Start with a trial agreement, then formalize.
  • 6
    Get a Written Agreement — Even a simple signed document between both parties covering: expense coverage, profit formula, minimum guarantee, machine return rights, and notice period. Consult a local business lawyer if needed.
Bottom Line
This deal is worth pursuing IF: all your expenses (including salary) are covered, you get a separate management fee, you have a written agreement, and you verify their billing claims. Under those conditions, you go from 5.22L → ~9.5–11L/month with zero out-of-pocket costs — essentially doubling your income while your machines get paid off by someone else's money.

Without those conditions in writing — don't move your machines.
